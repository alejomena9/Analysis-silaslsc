July 11

Back-three, attacking wing-backs
In One Line

Louisville's wing-backs stretch the field on principle, no matter who plays there — the rematch is about breaking their press before it sets.

A System, Not A Personnel Dependency

Louisville's main strength going into the rematch is their wing-back play. Regardless of who occupies those roles, both wing-backs have one clear job: stretch the opposing defense horizontally and force gaps to open centrally.

They follow two simple principles depending on where the space is. If the space is out wide, both wing-backs push to reach the byline. If the space is inside, both wing-backs instead prioritize generating width so that one or more of the interior midfielders can attack the gaps that open up in the rival back line. All of this is built on dominating the ball and dominating the positional picture of the match.

A High, Aggressive Defensive Identity

Defensively, Louisville commit to a very advanced back line with clear principles: press immediately after losing the ball, and if the ball can't be won back right away, either cut the move short with a tactical foul or hold their defensive shape until the team has fully recovered its position.

Prediction

The rematch is decided by tempo — break Louisville's press before it fully sets, and attack the space their high defensive line leaves behind it.
