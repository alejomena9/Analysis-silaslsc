April 1

High, overlapping full-backs
In One Line

Louisville live off crosses and second balls — winning those duels, and retreating as a full unit, is the difference in this game.

Crossing Is Their Weapon Of Choice

Louisville push both full-backs very high and constantly, and crossing is by far their most dangerous way of creating chances. That means real discipline is required against balls played in behind Lexington's own full-backs — Louisville will look to stretch the defense the same way they did against Rhode Island, pulling the back line apart until gaps open up wide enough for as many as six players to join the attack at once.

The Team Has To Retreat Together

The second key point is defensive shape on the retreat: when Lexington drops, it has to drop as a complete, coordinated unit. In the first meeting with Louisville — and again against San Antonio — Mike specifically did not complete his recovery run, which repeatedly left his side's full-back exposed to a 1v1 against the winger, or the near interior exposed to a 2v1. Jumping the press too early or too late creates exactly the gap Louisville is built to exploit.

Second Balls Decide Duels

Third, expect a heavy volume of crossed and long balls, which means second-ball situations will happen constantly — and whoever wins those second balls gains a real edge. We saw this directly in the first match: Aaron collected a loose rebound and scored from distance, and Louisville's own second goal that day came from exactly the same kind of loose-ball situation.

Prediction

Win the crosses, win the second balls, and retreat as a complete unit — those three details are what separate a controlled performance from a repeat of the first meeting.

01

Win the crosses

Discipline behind our full-backs; don't let the back line get stretched.

02

Win the second balls

Bodies around the drop zone on every long ball and cross.

03

Retreat as one unit

Every recovery run finished, no early or late jumps on the press.
