August 1

Direct build-up, minimal zone 2
In One Line

Monterrey skip zone two almost entirely and lean on a shaky left-back — pressing their exit and hunting his blind side is the way in.

A Very Direct Build-Up Pattern

Monterrey have a clear, repeatable pattern: everything starts in zone one, where they try to build with no more than three or four touches before looking for depth. They have almost no interest in holding the ball in zone two — in fact, they generate practically nothing there. The plan is simple: exit quickly from the back and look for the space behind the rival defense to go straight into zone three.

What A Good Press Does To Them

That directness has a real cost: when they face a high, well-coordinated press in zone one, they struggle badly to play out cleanly. Whether it's a forced long ball or a turnover inside the press itself, they typically end up losing possession. For Lexington, that's a clear opportunity to generate recoveries in high, dangerous zones — as long as the press is properly coordinated.

A Left-Back Who Loses His Man

Their most exposed individual weakness is the left-back, who consistently loses track of runners behind him. Against Phoenix, that exact issue produced three clear scoring chances in under five minutes purely from balls played behind him. On top of that, because their forwards sit so advanced and their back line drops so deep, they leave a lot of open space in zone fourteen — a zone Monterrey concede into frequently.

A Tactical Detail Worth Knowing

One interesting wrinkle: Monterrey's center-backs jump onto the opposing striker specifically to stop him from turning, but they aren't actually trying to win the ball — they're shadowing, not tackling. That means a target striker can receive with his back to goal fairly comfortably, even if he can't spin immediately.

Prediction

Look for balls played behind the left-back, occupy zone fourteen with real numbers, and take advantage of the space naturally left by a team that plays so long and with so little connection between its own lines.
