Structure

El Paso Locomotive defend in a 3-4-2-1 and attack in a 3-2-5. The wing-backs push as wide as possible while the interiors tuck in centrally, ready to burst diagonally into the wide channels of the final third.

Without the ball their high press is very well executed and forces mistakes — Lexington have to be clean in the first phase.

The Diagonal

Their core attacking pattern is the inside-to-outside diagonal. The left interior, Amando Moreno (#10), starts centrally and breaks into the channel behind the opposing full-back — usually on El Paso's left, with Gabi Torres (#16) holding the width outside him. The target is zone 16 before the back line can reorganise.

Lexington's Plan

Out of possession, a mid-block that protects the space behind our two pivots, with disciplined shifting across the pitch. In their build-up, take Tony Alfaro (#93) away — he's the one who plays through the inside lanes — and let Noah Dollenmayer (#15), the most limited on the ball, be the one who has it.

In possession, win it and break quickly into the space behind the wing-backs, above all behind Torres. With so many bodies committed forward, El Paso's centre-backs are left 1v1 — exactly the situation for Phillip Goodrum, who has seven goals in his last three matches.
