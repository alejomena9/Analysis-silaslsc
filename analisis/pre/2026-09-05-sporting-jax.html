Jacksonville's Structure

Jacksonville are an orderly, disciplined side that sets its block in midfield. With the ball they build in a 3-2-5: very high wing-backs, but a back three that stays tight and narrow.

Without it they shift between a 5-4-1 and a 5-2-3, depending on how high the opposing interior midfielders push.

Where The Block Breaks

A 3v3 press on Jacksonville's centre-backs forces errors — Sacramento's opener came from exactly that, a short build-up mishandled under pressure. Press with only two forwards and JAX play out with ease, as they did against Indy Eleven.

Once the press has done its job, the space is behind the double pivot. Braudílio, as a false 9 dropping into that zone, can have real impact if one good line-breaking pass cuts through the block.

Width On The Left

Using width with the full-back and winger on the same side puts Jacksonville's wing-back in a 2v1, the way Indy Eleven did. On the left, Greene and Epps are the pair for it, adding the inside-out diagonals Epps tends to make.

Tactical key · Sacramento's blueprint

Make Jacksonville move: intensity in possession, changes of tempo, the space behind the wing-backs in 1v1 or 2v1, and an organised press against a compact defence.

The Silver Bullet: Set Pieces

Jacksonville struggle badly with set pieces and balls into the box. Sacramento scored twice from corners in their 5–0 win, and Indy Eleven won it in stoppage time. It's this opponent's most repeatable weakness.

Risk to watch

Jacksonville's left centre-back is their most consistent long-ball distributor and triggers very fast transitions. Don't over-commit numbers forward without cover on set pieces or in open play.
