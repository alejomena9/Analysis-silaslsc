July 22

Possession, “U” build-up
In One Line

Oakland are excellent with the ball but tire under sustained possession — outlasting them, not trading blows, is how they were beaten last season.

Comfortable In Possession

Oakland Roots are a genuinely good possession team, comfortable in short combination play, especially down the flanks, and they build a clear "U" shape around the box as they work the ball forward. They almost always play out from the back, and if the short passing game gets pressed out, their left center-back has the quality to switch long directly to the center-forward or the left winger.

Most of their goals come from crosses into the box, and they commit a lot of numbers forward to make sure those crosses always have multiple targets to find.

Compact, Sometimes Too Compact

Defensively they're a very compact side that presses as a block and protects zone 14 carefully — they rarely allow passes through that zone, and if the press is beaten, they'll usually cut the move short with a tactical foul to reset their shape.

The trade-off is that they play so close together that they can be vulnerable to quick switches of play behind their full-backs, since shifting that tight defensive block across quickly is not their strength.

Prediction

Because Oakland are so comfortable with the ball, the smarter plan is not to fight fire with fire but to draw them into long spells of Lexington possession — tiring their defensive shifting until switches behind the full-backs become live options for crosses. That's exactly the approach that beat them last season, when long possessions and crosses from both flanks found runners arriving from deep — Cory and Joe then, and this year the mobility and box arrivals of Braudilio, plus Mike and Zengue's ability to reach the byline and cross, with Aaron, Firmino and Blaine encouraged to attack the box on those same crossed balls.
