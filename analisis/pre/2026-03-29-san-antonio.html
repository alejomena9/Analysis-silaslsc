March 29

3-4-3 / 5-4-1 defHome side, high press bursts
In One Line

San Antonio press in bursts and defend deep with numbers, but that same density leaves zone 14 open behind their marking midfielders.

Shape & Directness

San Antonio line up in a 3-4-3 with two interiors and two wide forwards when they have the ball, and drop into a 5-4-1 out of possession — two full-backs tuck all the way back to form a low, very compact block with heavy defensive volume.

In attack they are direct and fast, always looking to play behind the opposing back line, and their balance clearly favors the left. The left-back, wearing #4, consistently reaches the byline and delivers crosses, while the right-back, #13, rarely gets to the end line and instead tucks inside more like an auxiliary interior, mostly just floating crosses in from deeper positions.

Where The Danger Comes From

Because their left side carries more depth and repetition, that flank is where Lexington's right side will feel the most pressure — whoever plays there needs to be well set defensively and refuse to get turned, since San Antonio are constantly hunting the space behind that full-back. Cover from the next player inside matters a great deal here, since it lets the wide defender step without immediately conceding the space behind him.

On the right, San Antonio rarely attack with the same depth — they tend to advance to the final third and then either cross from deeper positions or cut inside to shoot from range, rather than getting to the byline.

The Gap Behind The Volume

San Antonio are a directness-first team: short spells of possession, quick combinations, fast transitions in both directions, and a low block that's heavy on bodies. But that same volume creates a blind spot — having many players in zone 14 does not mean that zone is actually well protected, and their intensity does not match their numbers there. A player who can consistently occupy the space at the back of their #55 — their defensive midfielder — can exploit that gap repeatedly.

Both center-backs, #21 and #17, are tall and lean heavily on their aerial ability, which also makes them overconfident — that confidence is precisely what allows strikers to find room for shots on goal in and around the box.

How San Antonio Will Set Up As Hosts

As the home side, expect a very high press in bursts — specifically the first and last 15 minutes of each half. That is their pattern in every home match, including against Portland. Against New Mexico they tried the same high start but had to drop off and defend deeper for the second half, which tells us the press is more a first-half weapon than a full-match approach.

Prediction

Putting a mobile runner like Alfredo directly behind San Antonio's holding midfielder should punish the zone-14 gap consistently, using his pace and long-range shooting to force San Antonio into an even deeper, more passive block.
