A Compact Block With A Hole In Zone 14

Monterey Bay line up in a 4-2-3-1 that is hard to break down through the middle. The cost is in the double pivot: the two midfielders cover so much ground side to side that they consistently arrive late to the pocket just outside the box. That's the recurring gap Lexington needs to find.

Their Outlet: The Long Ball Behind The Full-Back

With the ball, the centre-backs look first for a direct diagonal into the channel behind the opposing full-back — their clearest source of danger in transition, and the route Ilijah Paul lives on. Take it away and they settle for slow, low-yield possession that rarely reaches the final third with a real chance.

Lexington's Route In: Mobility Behind The Pivot

Because the pivot covers so much ground, one player dropping into the pocket isn't enough on its own — two moving players are. A midfielder rotating into Zone 14 while a second attacker drops to combine creates constant confusion over who picks up whom, and that is the direct route into Monterey Bay's core structural weakness.

Season Snapshot

Lexington arrive on two straight wins — 4–1 at Oakland and 6–0 against Orange County, both with a Goodrum hat trick — sitting 4th in the West with 37 points. Monterey Bay are bottom with 18 and winless since June 20. Lexington also won the first meeting 3–2 on August 1. The gap in the table is real, but the diagonal behind the full-back is exactly the kind of chance a struggling side can live on.

Game Plan

Monterey Bay's block is disciplined, but discipline that covers too much ground still leaves a door open. Attack the pocket with two moving players, and don't give them the diagonal in behind.

01

02

Risk · the long diagonal

The moment they win it, the centre-backs look in behind. Coordinated pressing triggers and disciplined cover, not isolated pressure from the front.
