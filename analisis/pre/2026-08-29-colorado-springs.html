Colorado's Structure

Out of possession Colorado set up in a compact 4-1-4-1, man-marking through midfield. In possession it becomes a 2-3-5: both full-backs push on, and the one on the far side from the ball tucks inside to form the base three with the holding midfielder, Speedy Williams.

The Crack Behind The Left-Back

With the left-back pushed that high, the space behind him is exposed the moment Colorado lose the ball. That is the pattern in their recent away defeats: they have more of the ball (21 shots in New Mexico, 56% possession in Phoenix) and still come home with nothing.

The route for Lexington is an immediate vertical ball into that channel on the turnover, with runners who can get there before he recovers.

Aerial Danger

Colorado generate heavy crossing and corner volume, and defender Isaiah Foster has scored this season from both a corner and a long throw-in. He has to be marked strictly on every set piece — and so do the other defenders who arrive late.

Game Plan

Colorado lost their last three away games with plenty of the ball. The crack was always in the same place — go there first, and don't lose Foster on the set pieces.
