In One Line

The pre-match reads were right, but a slow, passive Lexington couldn't punish them consistently — and San Antonio's superior intensity decided the game.

A Clear Intensity Gap

The biggest story of this match was the gap in intensity. San Antonio were sharp and committed — pressing hard, fighting for every loose ball, even shooting from range, and winning the vast majority of crossed and contested balls. Lexington, by contrast, looked passive and slow, struggling to break through San Antonio's lines for long stretches.

The Pocket We Identified Actually Opened Up

Even in a slow performance, Lexington still found the exact space flagged before kickoff — the pocket behind San Antonio's marking midfielders. Alfredo received well there, created room for himself, and was able to set up Philip for a shot that had a real chance of being the opener. The same overconfidence we'd noted in San Antonio's center-backs showed up here too: Philip had the clearest chance of the match off a second header when Marcos won the first ball back into the box, but the follow-up finish drifted just wide.

Where It Went Wrong

Lexington's passivity got worse as the game went on — the second half was noticeably slower and less intense than the first. San Antonio, meanwhile, only grew more consistent and aggressive, and that difference produced their second goal: a change of rhythm through midfield exposed Molloy and Ferri centrally, precisely because San Antonio's two full-backs were pushed so high and wide. With Lexington poorly set defensively in that moment, San Antonio's midfielder was left with a clean, unchallenged strike on goal.

Conclusion

The tactical picture from the pre-match report held up — the space existed exactly where it was expected to be. What decided the result was game intensity, not game plan: San Antonio simply played the match at a higher gear than Lexington did.
