July 25

High volume, both flanks
In One Line

Tulsa flood the box from wide areas all match — the way to hurt them is to win the ball and punish their thin defensive transition immediately.

Constant Attacking Volume

FC Tulsa are a dynamic, intense side that attacks with real volume — sending a huge number of players into the box from the flanks, mixing crosses and long balls in search of a numerical overload inside the area. Their recent games against El Paso and Colorado Springs show exactly this pattern: sustained danger generated for the full ninety minutes through sheer bodies committed forward.

The Cost Of Committing So Many Forward

That same commitment is also their weakness. With so many players pushed into the attack, their defensive transition suffers — winning the ball back and breaking quickly against them can do real damage before they reorganize.

Game Plan

This match is tailor-made for Goodrum and Brau — the whole plan hinges on Lexington's defense-to-attack transitions being fast, effective and clinical in front of goal. Marcus and Zengue need to be ready to run the space immediately, with Aaron, Firmino and Blaine arriving from deeper positions to finish. At the other end, expect a heavy dose of crosses, long balls and bodies in the box, so winning the aerial duels matters just as much. Fast transition, clinical finishing, and dominance in the air — that's the formula to get a result in Tulsa.
