The Molloy & Ferri Variable

Rotating Diamond
In One Line

Dropping the fixed double-6 gave Lexington's midfield the freedom to form a rotating diamond behind the opposing pivots — and that diamond is opening the flanks too.

Removing The Fixed Double-6

One of the coach's most important adjustments across the season has been eliminating the fixed double-6 in midfield. Ferri and Aaron no longer occupy the same defensive height at the same time — when one of them drops off to join the attack, the other stays to cover. That single change has freed up real space in the creative zone and allowed the team to generate more positional superiority right behind the opposing double pivot.

A More Mobile Forward Completes The Picture

Pairing that midfield adjustment with the arrival of a more mobile forward like Braudilio completes the picture. Behind the opposing marking midfielders, Lexington now forms a rotating diamond made up of the dropping 6, Firmino operating as a 10, the winger who tucks inside — usually Marcus — and Braudilio himself.

Interior Superiority Opens The Flanks

That interior superiority does more than just help build-up play centrally — it also frees up the flanks and allows for faster, more direct transitions once the interior midfielders push forward into the space that diamond creates.
