July 15

High press (leaky)
In One Line

New Mexico press hard but lose track of runners on crosses — the plan is cover their right-sided threat and attack their static back line with two clear targets.

An Intense But Breakable Press

New Mexico are an intense pressing side, trying to win the ball high up the pitch. But when that press is beaten, real gaps open up on either side of their midfield line, and the team becomes stretched and disjointed — often split into two disconnected halves.

Defensive Points

Their high press comes at a cost on crosses: their center-backs tend to ball-watch instead of tracking the runs of opposing forwards, which means they lose players and concede attacks and goals from exactly that kind of run. Their midfielders have the same issue — they don't communicate or track the runs of opposing midfielders, so rival attackers regularly find room to receive crosses inside the box.

Offensive Points

In attack, New Mexico look to hit the flanks with diagonal balls between center-back and full-back, constantly targeting the space behind the opposing full-back. Their center-back wearing #3 is particularly good at delivering these balls, and winger #5 is excellent at receiving them and creating danger. Most of their service comes in as driven, cut-back style balls rather than aerial crosses, aimed at zones their #10 attacks at the near post.

Suggestions

Defensively, the right-back spot needs real cover against balls in behind — bringing Brown in at left-back to help shore up that side is recommended. In attack, lean on driven crosses into the half-spaces with Braudilio as a second striker and Phillip as the out-and-out target man, giving the defense two different runners to track; using Zengue at right-back adds his long stride and quality service as another route to goal.
