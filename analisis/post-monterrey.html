August 1

3–2 (W)Direct build-up
In One Line

Both sides attacked the exact weaknesses flagged before kickoff — and a single substitution for width turned the whole match in Lexington's favor.

Monterrey's Goals Came From The Predicted Space

Monterrey built both of their goals by attacking one specific space. By avoiding risk in their own build-up, they made sure Lexington couldn't press them high, and from there they looked for a direct long ball into the space behind the right-back. That zone-three pocket was exactly the weakness flagged before the match.

Lexington's Comeback, Same Weaknesses, Different Side

Lexington's comeback came from attacking those same kinds of gaps on Monterrey's side of the pitch. The first goal was built on a well-organized press against Monterrey's own exit from the back — Lexington won the second ball, which is precisely where a team that plays this directly is most likely to break down. The second goal simply confirmed the pattern from the pre-match read: Monterrey defend more weakly on their left, so Lexington attacked that side and the cross found its way into the box.

The Substitution That Changed The Match

The moment that really decided the game was structural. Bringing on Tariq gave Lexington genuine width for the first time, which forced Monterrey to stretch their back line to cover that flank. Stretching that line left Epps isolated against his marker — exactly the situation he thrives in, whether cutting inside or attacking the far post. The third goal was a direct consequence of that same insistence, arriving off a corner rebound.

Conclusion

With the result already in Lexington's favor, the match called for a different kind of control late on. An additional midfielder off the bench would have added possession and support for the long runs Goodrum and Molloy were already making, rather than leaving the team exposed in transition for the closing stretch.
