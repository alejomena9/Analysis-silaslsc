An Early Lead, A Protected Base

Lexington started with Noble Okello in midfield next to Ferri and Molloy — the base that lets the midfield rotate without leaving the back line exposed. The opener came early: a quick free kick from deep sent Zengue down the right, his effort was blocked, and Goodrum finished the rebound at 22'.

Oakland's Spell, Lexington's Structure

Oakland's coach Ryan Martin changed the approach at half-time — more direct, more aggressive in transition — and it worked for a spell: Peter Wilson headed in McCabe's cross at 52' and the Roots dominated the ball from the 50th to the 75th minute.

But the numbers show what the structure did with that pressure: eleven Oakland corners, fourteen shots, and only three on target.

The Last Twelve Minutes

The more Oakland pushed, the more space opened behind them. At 78' Zengue and Ferri combined and Goodrum finished through a crowded box. At 83' Oakland's ninth corner turned into a Lexington goal: Ferri's looping ball and Epps running onto it unopposed. At 87', on another counter, Goodrum completed his hat trick with a diving header from Epps' cross.

Conclusion

Oakland did what a chasing home side does and paid for it. Lexington didn't need the ball — the Okello in the base held the middle through Oakland's best spell, and every time the Roots committed bodies forward, Ferri, Epps and Goodrum were ready to go the other way.
