Team Tactics

Structural shifts in how Lexington sets up as a team — not one player, but the shape and roles around them.

02 / Team Tactics
Team Tactics

The Molloy & Ferri Variable

Rotating Diamond
What Changed Dropping the fixed double-6 — Ferri and Aaron no longer sit at the same height at all times, one covers while the other steps forward.
Effect Combined with a more mobile Braudílio up top, Lexington now forms a rotating diamond (dropping 6, Firmino as a 10, a tucking winger, and Braudílio) behind the opposing markers.

Conclusion — The interior superiority this creates also opens the flanks for faster, more direct transitions.

Team Tactics

3 Games, One Week

Efficiency
What Changed Territorial control held up in all three matches — LSC dictated tempo, reached the final third consistently, and delivered regular service into the box, home or away.
Effect Conversion never matched the process: 3 goals across 3 games, capped by a 1.25 xG performance vs. Colorado Springs that still ended in a loss.

Conclusion — Lexington's problem isn't reaching the final third. It's finishing once it gets there.

High press, diagonal overloads
Left interior Moreno — underlaps into zone 16 Back three — exposed 1v1 once the press is beaten #93 Alfaro — best passer, set-piece threat
Attack Their identity is the inside-out diagonal: the left interior tucks in, then bursts behind the opposing full-back into the wide channel, with the wing-back overlapping outside him. It's a genuinely dangerous, repeatable pattern — not a gimmick.

Direct build-up

vs. Phoenix Rising FC

August 8

W 1–0
Tarik Scott — width off the bench Epps — freed by the switch Burks & Brown — shut down transitions
Positional build-up
Attack Lexington controlled the game for 45 minutes without reward — occupying all five lanes but disconnected in the box, with Goodrum repeatedly left isolated on crosses. The fix came from the bench: Tarik Scott's introduction in the 59th added the width the starting XI lacked, opening space behind Phoenix's holding midfielder that Epps exploited. The goal followed four minutes later.
Defense Burks and Brown were the platform that never wavered — absorbing every Phoenix attack and delaying transitions all match. Phoenix finished the game without a single shot on target.

Conclusion — A deserved win built on control and a clean sheet, but the box-connection problem was solved reactively rather than by design — that fix needs to happen before kickoff, not after 59 minutes.
